# Revision 18: identify the active attacker in early crossfire

The baseline enters evasion when two geometrically exposed mechs are nearby,
even when one has been facing and moving elsewhere. The experiment narrows
engagement focus only in the first 75 seconds with at least 12 mechs alive and
exactly one credible attacker. A passer must remain at least 90 m away,
facing over 60 degrees away, with no closing motion or firing evidence for
at least 0.6 seconds of continuous observations. New threats start out credible.

An observed turn, charge, approach, missing track, or attributed attack
restores the more cautious policy. The positions of all known mechs still
inform terrain, escape, and projectile checks. Firing focus changes only for
an otherwise favorable duel against the one credible attacker.

In a physical feedback case, an enemy previously classified as a passer turns
and actually fires. The classifier restores crossfire within one observation
at 0.9 s, while the mech is farther than 114 m away. In two turning cases the
candidate survives longer, but both versions eventually die. Two quiet cases
produce essentially the same kills and worse remaining HP for the candidate,
with more remaining energy. These narrow cases support the transitions and
limits, without establishing a quiet-fight advantage. The official recording
contains no frames with multiple exposed mechs within the baseline's duel gate.

All 129 local tests passed, including nine policy tests and two physical
feedback tests. The official checker answered 300/300 on time, with maximum
8.6 ms. Local crowded and growing-body maxima were 17.30 and 21.46 ms.
